## General feedback
In summary, the analysis of the augMENTOR profiles reveals distinct strengths and weaknesses across cognitive and 21st-century skills. The analysis of the performance data reveals that both UPAT_A and UPAT_B profiles exhibit strong cognitive skills aligned with the course's demands for understanding and applying ICT in education. However, critical thinking skills show variability and inconsistency across both profiles, while creativity is moderate, particularly for UPAT_A. UPAT_B learners demonstrate strengths in collaboration and creativity but face challenges in autonomy and digital fluency, which are essential for effective engagement with the course content and technology integration.

The proposed policies address these identified needs by recommending targeted interventions that scaffold critical thinking and creativity development, enhance digital literacy, and foster learner autonomy. Learner-focused policies emphasize structured activities, formative assessments, and technology integration tailored to each profile's characteristics. Educator-focused strategies provide actionable guidance for lesson design, feedback, and inclusive practices aligned with the TESA framework phases, ensuring that future courses are responsive to learner profiles. Collectively, these policies are projected to improve learner engagement, deepen skill acquisition, and enhance overall educational outcomes within the context of ICT integration in education.

## Discussion about learners' profiles
### augMENTOR profile: UPAT_A
**Characteristics:** Confident and highly autonomous individuals with strong competence and solid digital literacy, particularly skilled in digital teaching practices.

**Description:** The UPAT_A profile represents learners who exhibit high levels of autonomy, competence, and relatedness. A large proportion of this group reports “high” to “very high” autonomy, reflecting their independence and self-direction in both learning and teaching contexts. Competence is equally strong, with a significant number scoring in the top two tiers. Relatedness is also a key strength, suggesting strong social bonds and a collaborative mindset. In digital teaching skills, UPAT_A learners show strong agreement with their ability to implement classroom activities using Web 2.0 tools and digital platforms like Moodle or e-Class. They are confident in integrating TPACK principles (Technological Pedagogical Content Knowledge) into their teaching strategies. Furthermore, they excel in supporting students' digital collaboration and learning, and in using digital environments for assessment. These learners are notably familiar with various digital learning tools tailored to their subject matter. Overall, UPAT_A profiles indicate tech-savvy educators or learners, with a progressive, independent approach to teaching and learning, coupled with robust self-belief in their capabilities.
### augMENTOR profile: UPAT_B
**Characteristics:** Supportive and socially aware learners who value inclusivity and collaboration, though showing room for growth in autonomy and digital fluency.

**Description:** The UPAT_B profile highlights individuals who report moderate levels of autonomy and competence, with relatedness being a strong point. This suggests that while they may not always take the lead, they thrive in collaborative environments and are deeply connected to their peers and learning communities. Their confidence in using digital tools for teaching is more varied. A larger percentage express disagreement or neutrality when asked about their ability to apply digital learning strategies or tools effectively. This may indicate a need for further support or training in integrating digital tools into pedagogical practice. Nonetheless, many still recognize the importance of technology and show some agreement in their ability to support students with digital tools. While they may not rate themselves as highly autonomous or digitally skilled as UPAT_A learners, UPAT_B individuals tend to be reflective, cooperative, and socially conscious, often aligning with values of inclusivity and group success.

## Policy recommendations for supporting learners

These policies aim to enhance cognitive depth and innovative capacities aligned with the course's focus on integrating ICT in education.

**Guidelines on the recommendation:**
- **Enhanced Critical Thinking Modules**: Structured problem-solving and inquiry-based tasks should be integrated to systematically develop critical thinking, aligned with TESA Phase E (Activities for Implementation in the Classroom).
- **Creativity-Boosting Collaborative Projects**: Technology-supported group projects should be designed to stimulate creativity and peer interaction, leveraging digital teaching practices (TESA Phase E).
- **Autonomy-Supportive Learning Paths**: Personalized learning activities with flexible pacing and digital resources should be provided to capitalize on learners' autonomy and digital skills (TESA Phase A and E).
- **Formative Digital Assessments**: Frequent, technology-enabled formative assessments should be implemented to monitor and guide critical thinking and creativity development (TESA Phase F: Evaluation).
- **Integration of Emerging Technologies**: Use of innovative ICT tools should be promoted to deepen pedagogical understanding and application (TESA Phase A and D).
#### #2 Supporting learners with supportive and socially aware learners who value inclusivity and collaboration, though showing room for growth in autonomy and digital fluency.

**Guidelines on the recommendation:**
